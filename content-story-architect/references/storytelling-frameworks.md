# Storytelling Frameworks

These are bundled, predefined frameworks. Treat them as read-only. Select the framework that best serves the content; do not force a piece into one when a hybrid or experiential approach is more appropriate.

## Contents

- Traditional / Linear Story
- In Medias Res / Start in the Middle
- Viral: Question → Journey → Answer
- Educational / Value-Driven
- Credibility-Led Breakdown
- HSTSS: Personal Transformation

## Traditional / Linear Story Framework

- **Purpose:** Tell a chronological story that builds toward an outcome or realization.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Setup (Hook + Context)** — Establish the starting situation, goal, and central question.
  2. **Conflict (Rising Action)** — Introduce tension and develop it through obstacles, attempts, or consequences.
  3. **Turn (Climax)** — Reach the decisive action, result, discovery, or realization.
  4. **Payoff (Resolution)** — Resolve the opening tension and reveal the outcome or meaning.
- **Best fit:** Chronological personal stories, process videos, challenges, project stories, and case studies where the sequence of events and rising tension matter.
- **Key rule:** Every stage must change the situation or the audience's understanding.

## In Medias Res / Start-in-the-Middle Framework

- **Purpose:** Begin with a compelling fragment from later in the story, then reveal how it happened and what it means.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Drop-In (Moment, Question, or Result)** — Open with an unresolved fragment whose context or meaning is missing.
  2. **Rewind (Setup + Context)** — Return to what led there and why it matters.
  3. **Build (Rising Action)** — Progress back toward the opening through events, complications, or discoveries.
  4. **Payoff (Return + Resolution)** — Complete or recontextualize the opening and resolve its central question.
- **Best fit:** Personal stories, process videos, challenges, or case studies where a later moment, question, or result creates a stronger entry point than the chronological setup.
- **Key rule:** The Drop-In may reveal what happened, but it must leave the audience wanting to understand how, why, or what it means.

## Viral Framework (Question → Journey → Answer)

- **Purpose:** Create engaging, curiosity-driven content designed to hold attention and build anticipation toward an answer or resolution.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Question (Hook + Promise)** — Establish a thought-provoking question or make a provocative or controversial claim that implies one.
  2. **Journey (Constant Progression)** — Take the audience on a journey with constant, felt progression toward the answer or resolution.
  3. **Complication (Conflict + Escalation)** — Introduce an obstacle, contradiction, or discovery that makes the answer uncertain, builds tension, and makes the ending satisfying or the resolution feel worth it.
  4. **Answer (Payoff + Meaning)** — Resolve the opening question with an earned conclusion, result, or realization.
- **Best fit:** Investigations, experiments, challenges, explainers, analyses, and idea-driven videos where discovering the answer is the primary source of momentum.
- **Key rule:** Every beat must advance, complicate, or partially answer the central question so the audience continually feels closer to resolution.

## Educational / Value-Driven Framework

- **Purpose:** Turn useful knowledge into clear, engaging content that helps the audience understand, do, or decide something.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Promise (Hook + Dream Outcome)** — Establish what the audience will learn or gain, why it matters, and use the Hook-Line-Sinker technique to pull them in with a Curiosity Loop.
  2. **Context (Problem + Knowledge Gap)** — Clarify the starting point, obstacle, misconception, or missing knowledge.
  3. **Teach (Value Loops + Progression)** — Deliver ideas or steps in a deliberate order, using Value Loops so each section provides context, application, and framing while building on what came before.
  4. **Apply (Examples + Demonstration)** — Show how the information works through practical examples, evidence, or scenarios.
  5. **Payoff (Takeaway + Transformation)** — Fulfill the opening promise with a clear conclusion and what the audience can now understand, do, or decide.
- **Best fit:** Tutorials, explainers, advice, breakdowns, lessons, and value-driven content where useful understanding or application is the primary payoff.
- **Key rule:** Do not merely present information; build toward a clear change in the audience's understanding or ability and why it matters for them.

## Credibility-Led Breakdown Framework

- **Purpose:** Turn an outcome, problem, or question the audience already cares about into a useful breakdown that establishes trust in the creator and gives viewers a reason to return for more.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Hook (Desire + Curiosity)** — Lead with a desired outcome, recognizable problem, or compelling question. Make the audience want to understand what happened, why it worked, or how it could help them.
  2. **Credibility (Experience + Trust)** — Briefly establish why your perspective is worth hearing through relevant credentials, firsthand experience, demonstrated work, or results.
  3. **Solution Preview (Promise + Direction)** — Clarify the explanation, method, or insight you are about to reveal and how it connects to the opening question.
  4. **Re-Hook (Entertainment + Contrast)** — Use a relevant clip, humorous interpretation, or alternate take to renew attention. When it presents a false lead, reveal why that explanation is incomplete and transition into the actual solution.
  5. **Breakdown (Insight + Demonstration)** — Deliver the promised solution through specific points, examples, evidence, or demonstrations. Use Value Loops so each point builds understanding and substantiates your credibility.
  6. **Personal Application (Relevance + Payoff)** — Show what the viewer can understand, do, or approach differently and why it matters to their own goals. Establish personal relevance early when helpful, then reinforce it through the payoff so the value of returning to your perspective is clear.
- **Best fit:** Expert commentary, case studies, creative or technical breakdowns, process explanations, and advice where earning trust in the creator is central to turning viewer interest into an ongoing audience relationship.
- **Key rule:** Credentials earn initial trust; the breakdown must justify it. Fulfill the opening promise and make its relevance to the viewer unmistakable.

## HSTSS Framework (Personal Transformation)

- **Purpose:** Turn a personal experience into an emotionally engaging story that leads to a meaningful realization or change in belief.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Hook (Curiosity + Idea Collision)** — Open with a compelling tension, contradiction, or collision between opposing ideas or identities that makes the audience want to understand the story.
  2. **Stakes (Conflict + Emotion)** — Reveal the moment when everything could be lost, why the experience mattered, and the emotions that made it personally significant. Use humility and relatability to set up the Turn/Pivotal Moment.
  3. **Turn (Discovery + Trust)** — Introduce the discovery, decision, or realization that changed the direction or meaning of the story. The lesson is baked into this moment, building authority, respect, and trust with the audience.
  4. **Scene (Sensory Experience)** — Dramatize the pivotal moment through concrete details, emotion, and the Five Senses Framework so the audience can experience it with you.
  5. **Shatter (Mic Drop + Belief Shift)** — Deliver the mic-drop realization: what you want the audience to understand or take away, the belief being shattered, and the meaning that leaves a lasting impact.
- **Best fit:** Personal stories, origin stories, creator journeys, speeches, case studies, and lessons built around lived experience where emotional meaning is the primary payoff.
- **Key rule:** The story is the doorway; the meaning is what you lead the audience into when they go through it.

### HSTSS Cliff Notes

#### Hook

- Great openings arouse curiosity.
- Capture attention immediately, then deepen the opening hook until it is resolved.
- Idea Collision places two seemingly incompatible ideas, identities, or situations beside each other.
- Examples: “Religious Leader vs. Drunken Man” and “Jesus and the Woman at the Well.”

#### Stakes

- Bring in humility and relatability.
- Great storytellers bring themselves into the story to connect with the audience.
- What makes a story meaningful is not only what happened—it is the perspective and framing brought to it.
- Describe the genuine emotion: shame, guilt, anger, sadness, hope, happiness, love, or something else.
- Set up the pivot into the Turn.

#### Turn

- The turning point contains the discovery or lesson.
- Focus the audience on what changed or made the desired outcome possible.
- This is where the storyteller can build authority, respect, and trust.

#### Scene

- Use the Five Senses Framework and supporting scene-construction techniques.
- Make the pivotal moment concrete and visualizable.
- Stories should not only appeal to logic; the audience should feel the experience.

#### Shatter

- Shatter an outdated belief or break through the idea blocking the audience.
- Deliver the mic-drop realization or lesson.
- Clarify what the audience should know or take away and demonstrate how it applies to them.
- The story creates entry; the meaning creates impact.
