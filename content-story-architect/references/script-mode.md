# Script Mode

Write or review content at the level the user requests: talking points, outline, partial script, full script, or visual/voiceover sequence.

## Create

1. Confirm the content's promise or intended experience from existing context.
2. Select or honor the storytelling framework.
3. Apply the five-step Scriptwriting System at the appropriate depth.
4. Use progression mechanics selectively to build momentum, value, tension, immersion, or payoff.
5. Match the requested delivery style: focused talking points, natural talking head, polished narration, cinematic voiceover, or another specified form.
6. Keep visual action and spoken narration meaningfully related when writing audiovisual work.
7. Provide only the requested artifact. Do not automatically add titles, captions, shot lists, or explanations unless useful or requested.

## Review

Review a script against its own intent and maturity. Examine:

- Promise and audience expectation
- Framework fit and structural integrity
- Hook and introduction
- Progression, sequencing, conflict, or value delivery
- Voice, clarity, concision, and speakability
- Payoff and ending
- Visual/voiceover relationship when applicable

Prioritize findings by impact. Cite exact lines or beats. Distinguish structural problems from stylistic preferences.

## Revise

- Preserve approved language and surrounding structure.
- Change only the requested scope unless a dependency makes another small change necessary; explain that dependency.
- Do not flatten idiosyncratic voice into generic creator language.
- For loose-bullet delivery, avoid over-scripting the middle. Hooks and closers may be more exact while the body remains conversational.
- When multiple valid rewrites exist, offer alternatives only if the choice materially affects voice or strategy.
