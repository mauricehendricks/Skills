# Hook Lab

Create or refine the smallest opening that earns attention and accurately promises the content that follows.

## Workflow

1. Establish the subject, intended audience, content intent, platform, and actual payoff from available context.
2. Identify the one central question or anticipation the opening should create. For experiential work, identify the intended entry experience instead.
3. Choose a suitable approach from the hook reference or use an original approach when the content calls for it.
4. Develop visual, spoken, and text channels together. They may complement rather than duplicate one another, but must not create conflicting promises.
5. Generate alternatives that are strategically distinct, not superficial synonyms.
6. Explain the meaningful tradeoff among options when offering more than one.
7. Refine the selected direction and run the Hook Writing Checklist.

## Output behavior

- Lead with usable hooks, then give concise rationale.
- Include visual and text treatment when those channels matter.
- Preserve the creator's natural voice and desired level of polish.
- Keep the hook proportionate to its format and move into substance once it has done its job.
- Never fabricate controversy, proof, statistics, results, or authority.
- Do not use a curiosity gap the content cannot resolve.
- When auditing a hook, distinguish clarity, curiosity, relevance, channel alignment, and promise integrity.

For a pure artifact, music piece, montage, or mood-first work, do not require one explicit spoken question. Judge whether the opening creates the intended attraction through imagery, sound, movement, atmosphere, or emotion.
