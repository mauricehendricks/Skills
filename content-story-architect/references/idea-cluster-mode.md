# Idea Cluster Mode

Use Idea Cluster for content ideation and batching. Take one concept, observation, topic, experience, opinion, question, experiment, or rough video idea; identify the simpler idea the creator is actually exploring; align on that interpretation when needed; then generate 3–5 meaningfully distinct standalone content concepts.

The intended progression is:

> 1 seed idea → 1 clear Core Question or Core Idea → creator alignment → 3–5 standalone concepts

The concepts should feel like siblings, not clones. A person who consumes all of them should receive a different perspective, story, question, insight, experience, emotional interpretation, practical application, or conceptual takeaway from each.

## Do not confuse clustering with optimization

Idea Cluster does not generate multiple hooks, titles, captions, thumbnails, edits, pacing variations, or lightly reworded versions of one script. It is not primarily an A/B-testing or algorithm-duplication mode.

Do not create volume by:

- splitting one argument into superficial pieces;
- changing only the wording, hook, or format;
- forcing trend formats or listicles;
- inventing vague philosophical interpretations;
- stretching the central idea until concepts no longer feel related.

Before including a concept, ask: “Would someone reasonably consider this a different piece of content?” Exclude it if it substantially overlaps another concept. Prefer three excellent concepts to three excellent concepts plus two fillers.

## Phase 1 — Intent Alignment

Unless the user has already stated the underlying intent or Core Question explicitly, return only the following:

### Seed Idea

Briefly restate what the creator brought in. Preserve its specificity and point of view without over-explaining it.

### What I Think the Core Intent Is

Concise interpretation of what the creator is fundamentally trying to explore, understand, demonstrate, communicate, question, or reveal beneath the current framing.

### Suggested Core Question / Core Idea

State the smallest useful zoom-out from the seed, usually about one conceptual level above it. Make the intent simpler and broad enough to support multiple legitimate pieces without making it more intellectual, poetic, or abstract than necessary.

Then ask the creator to confirm or refine the interpretation. Prefer a momentum-preserving prompt such as:

> I think you’re exploring X, and I’d phrase the Core Question as Y. Is that right?

Do not generate the cluster before alignment.

### Alignment exceptions

- If the creator explicitly supplies the underlying intent or Core Question and asks for a cluster, acknowledge it and proceed directly to Phase 2. Do not force redundant confirmation.
- If the seed already functions as a strong Core Question, keep it rather than abstracting it again.
- If 2–3 interpretations are genuinely plausible, present no more than three concise options, recommend the strongest one, and ask which matches the creator’s intent. Do not generate multiple full clusters.
- Avoid open-ended questions such as “What do you mean?” unless the input is impossible to interpret. Make the best interpretation first.

## Identify the core accurately

The seed may be a story, experiment, hook, anecdote, question, or technical observation rather than the true central idea. Ask internally: “What is this actually about beneath its current framing?”

For example:

- Seed: “I spent two hours trying to create space in my vocals. Here’s what happened.”
- Underlying intent: Explore how space changes how a vocal feels and functions in a production.
- Core Question: “What effect does space create in vocal production?”

Do not inflate this into a distant theme such as “How absence shapes emotional perception in art.” Conversely, if the seed is already “Why do creative limitations sometimes make better work?”, use it as the Core Question.

## Phase 2 — Generate the Idea Cluster

After alignment—or immediately when the creator explicitly provided the Core Question—return:

### Seed Idea

A concise restatement of the creator’s input.

### Underlying Intent

The aligned interpretation of what the creator is exploring.

### Core Question / Core Idea

The aligned smallest useful zoom-out.

Then generate 3–5 numbered concepts. Do not force five. Stop as soon as another framing would be repetitive, derivative, weak, generic, cosmetically different, or artificially philosophical.

For each concept, use:

## 01 — [Perspective Name]

**Perspective:** A short angle label, such as Personal Experiment, Practical Process, Emotional, Perceptual, Contrarian, or Cultural.

**Framing:** A concise working title, premise, or hookable expression of the content idea.

**Core Premise:** One or two sentences explaining what this standalone piece actually explores.

**Central Tension:** The curiosity, conflict, contrast, surprise, uncertainty, or emotional movement that drives this framing.

**Hook Direction:** One strong example of how it could begin. Treat this as directional, not a final optimized hook; Hook Lab can refine it later.

**Story / Value Payoff:** What the audience leaves with, such as insight, entertainment, emotion, knowledge, transformation, inspiration, practical value, curiosity, or a changed perspective.

## Create real creative range

Framings may operate at different abstraction levels and use personal, practical, technical, emotional, philosophical, conceptual, educational, observational, contrarian, narrative, experiential, perceptual, or cultural perspectives.

Vary abstraction only when it reveals a meaningful new dimension of the Core Question. Every framing must have a plainly explainable connection to it; if that connection requires several logical leaps, exclude the framing.

Preserve a strong original seed as one of the concepts when it is already compelling. Expand around it rather than assuming it must be replaced.

Preserve the creator’s point of view. Prefer concepts grounded in what they experienced, noticed, believe, made, failed at, were surprised by, changed their mind about, know unusually well, or are actively questioning. Do not automatically turn every idea into generic advice, a tutorial, or a listicle.

## Batch Potential

Finish with a concise assessment of:

- which concepts can share research, notes, a filming session, production setup, location, source footage, creative experience, project, or subject matter;
- which concepts require different preparation or source material;
- which one or two concepts appear strongest and why;
- whether any concept is deliberately more experimental.

Shared production efficiency is useful, but every concept must still stand on its own.

For a cinematic short, documentary, commercial, film, heavily authored artifact, or other high-production work, identify adjacent standalone ideas inspired by the project when appropriate. Do not automatically multiply the original artifact into alternate versions.

Do not write full scripts or detailed outlines unless the creator explicitly asks to develop a selected concept. Route later development to Concept, Hook Lab, Script, or Audit & Revision as appropriate.

## Invocation cues

Recognize Idea Cluster requests including:

- “Idea Cluster this.”
- “Turn this into an Idea Cluster.”
- “What is the bigger idea here?”
- “Give me different perspectives on this.”
- “How many videos can I get out of this idea?”
- “Give me 3–5 framings of this.”
- “Cluster: [idea]”
- “Batch this idea.”

The alignment checkpoint exists to expand the idea the creator actually meant, not merely the interpretation the model found most interesting.
