# Audit & Revision Mode

Diagnose how a concept, outline, hook, script, shot list, or finished piece works. Use the playbook as evidence-backed lenses, not as a universal compliance checklist.

## Establish audit scope

Before judging the piece, determine:

- The user's requested scope: full audit or focused audit
- Artifact stage: idea, draft, production plan, filmed, published, or immutable
- Dominant intent: informational, narrative, persuasive, experiential, or blended
- Intended platform and format when known
- Available evidence and material limitations

Do not invent missing packaging, visuals, delivery, analytics, or audience response. Mark them as unavailable.

## Diagnose

1. State the inferred intent and primary framework, plus secondary influences when useful.
2. Judge the piece according to the relevant criteria. Weight criteria rather than treating every technique as mandatory.
3. Cite exact lines, scenes, beats, timestamps, or visual choices for material findings.
4. Identify strengths before prescribing changes so successful decisions are preserved.
5. Separate:
   - Clear weaknesses
   - Intentional creative tradeoffs
   - Growth opportunities that are not artistic necessities
   - Missing evidence that cannot be assessed
6. Prioritize the few issues with the largest effect.
7. End with an overall verdict and transferable lesson.

## Intent-sensitive evaluation

- **Informational:** weight clarity, novelty, Value Loops, application, sequencing, and transformation.
- **Narrative:** weight desire, conflict, escalation, turns, scenes, progression, and earned payoff.
- **Persuasive:** weight relevance, credibility, objections, desire, proof, and promise integrity.
- **Experiential:** weight entry experience, mood, originality, rhythm, aesthetic coherence, emotion, and memorability. Do not require a conventional story or explicit hook.

## Revision behavior

If the user requests revision, propose the smallest changes that address the diagnosis. Preserve the successful intent, structure, and language. If the piece is finished or the user requests audit only, do not rewrite it; provide lessons for future work.

Use scores only when the user asks for them or when comparison requires a consistent scale. Prefer precise qualitative judgment over false numerical certainty.
