# 5-Step Scriptwriting System

Develop content from its initial promise through its final payoff while ensuring the delivered experience meets or exceeds the expectations created by its packaging.

## Governing principle — Expectation vs. Reality

- **Core idea:** What the audience thinks is going to happen versus what actually happens.
- **Expectation** is what the idea, title, thumbnail, and opening lead the audience to anticipate.
- **Reality** is what the content actually delivers.
- **Reality ≥ Expectation** creates satisfaction and trust.
- **Reality < Expectation** creates disappointment.

## System

### 1. Packaging (Promise + Click Confirmation)

- Align the idea, title, thumbnail where applicable, and opening hook around one clear expectation.
- Meeting the audience's baseline expectation is mandatory without exception. Immediately confirm that they are receiving what the packaging promised.
- Exceed that expectation by making the delivered reality more valuable, compelling, or surprising than anticipated. This positive gap between expectation and reality is what hooks the viewer.

### 2. Outline (Substance + Structure)

- Select the appropriate Storytelling Framework and define the central promise, question, or desired outcome.
- Build a unique and meaningful sequence of beats that allows Reality to exceed Expectation.
- Prioritize novel points and real substance rather than repeating what the audience already knows.
- Distill each point, use deeper examples, and develop the **What, Why, and How** where relevant.

### 3. Intro (Context + Curiosity Loop)

- **Establish Immediate Context** — Clearly confirm what the content is about.
- **Establish the Common Take or Belief** — Restate what the audience likely already believes to create a baseline expectation.
- **Introduce the Contrarian Approach or Perspective** — Challenge that baseline with a fresh perspective, creating contrast and opening a Curiosity Loop.
- **Give Proof when relevant** — Establish why the audience should trust the perspective or continue watching.
- **Share the Plan when relevant** — Preview how the content will explore, demonstrate, or prove its promise.

Treat this as a useful intro configuration, not a requirement for every creative artifact. Experiential and cinematic openings may establish interest through image, sound, atmosphere, action, or emotion.

### 4. Body (Progression + Value Delivery)

- **Follow the selected framework** — Develop the story, argument, lesson, or experience according to its chosen structure.
- **Lead with Novel Value** — For point-based content, the first point should not simply repeat something the audience already knows. Deliver meaningful value early so Reality begins exceeding Expectation.
- **Use Value Loops** — Develop each applicable point through:
  - **Context — What is it?** Explain the idea as clearly, concisely, and digestibly as possible. Give the audience only the background required to understand the point.
  - **Application — How can it be used?** Demonstrate how the audience can apply the idea through relevant examples, actions, or scenarios contextualized to them.
  - **Framing — Why does it matter?** Zoom out and reconnect the point to the larger story, problem, or desired outcome. Clarify why the information matters and how it moves the audience toward the overall payoff.
- **Create the Wave** — Alternate between the **Big Picture → Tactics, Examples, or Scenes → Big Picture** to maintain clarity and relevance.
- **Re-hook between beats** — Connect the current payoff to the next question, complication, or promise.
- **Sequence deliberately** — Make each point build on what came before and position the strongest material where it creates the greatest impact.

### 5. Outro (Payoff + Closure)

- **Deliver the Payoff** — Close the primary Curiosity Loop and fulfill the promise established by the packaging.
- **Reinforce the Resolution** — Revisit the pain point, problem, or desired outcome and clarify what was solved, learned, or changed.
- **Confirm Reality Beat Expectation** — Remind the audience, through the result or a concise recap, that the content delivered more than the original setup promised.
- **Leave on a High Note** — End with the strongest useful, emotional, or resonant moment rather than allowing the content to trail off.
- **Summarize only when helpful** — Use a recap when it improves clarity, but avoid unnecessarily repeating the entire piece.

## Best use and key rule

- **Best used:** Any content that benefits from deliberate packaging, outlining, structured progression, and a defined payoff.
- **Key rule:** The system must serve the chosen story rather than forcing every piece of content into the same style. Packaging, progression, and payoff must remain aligned.

## Cliff Notes

### Packaging

- Check alignment between the idea, title, thumbnail, and opening hook.
- Ask what the audience should expect after clicking or choosing to watch.
- Meet that baseline immediately, then exceed it.

### Outline

- Build around unique, interesting, and substantive points.
- Avoid merely repeating common knowledge.
- Distill ideas and support them with deeper examples.
- Layer the **What, Why, and How**.

### Intro

- Immediate context prevents confusion.
- A common belief establishes the baseline.
- A contrarian perspective creates contrast.
- Proof can establish authority, respect, and trust.
- A plan can create ordered anticipation when appropriate.

### Body

- The first point should prove that the audience will receive something new.
- Use the **Second-Best-First / 2-1-3-4 approach** when appropriate, preserving the strongest material for a later position.
- Build each teaching point through **Context → Application → Framing**.
- Create the Wave through **Big Picture → Tactics → Big Picture → Tactics**.
- Use Re-Hooks to connect the end of one point to the beginning of the next.

### Outro

- Leave on a high note.
- Reinforce the pain point or desired outcome that was resolved.
- Confirm that Reality met or exceeded Expectation.

### Bonus Tip: Native Embeds

- When creating the outline, identify where to highlight a free resource as the solution, framing the pain point and presenting your resource as the fix.
  - Introduce the pain point, then offer the solution.
  - Or quickly cover a very in-depth framework that people may not be able to absorb fully, then offer your free resource to speed up implementation. Make them think they need your resource.
- Use a Native Embed only when it genuinely improves implementation or understanding. Do not force a commercial resource into a piece that does not need it.
